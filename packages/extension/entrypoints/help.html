Capturing design with Luci

Luci turns what you pick on a live page into a design.md file your AI coding agent can use. Everything stays on your machine.

Three steps

  1. Pick a tool and click. Hover the page: the label shows the element's tag and size. Click what you want.
  2. Adjust the selection. The element stays selected. ↑ Wider selects the element around it, ↓ Narrower the one inside it. The ↑ ↓ keys do the same.
  3. Choose an output. This one captures the item; The whole system captures everything like it on the page. Then copy the file, download it, or add it to the basket.

The tools

  • Component: a button, card, link or field: its parts, tokens, motion and states (hover, focus, active, disabled), and how each image in it is shown (size, crop, corners, effects, tints over it). The whole system adds its variants on the page.
  • Text: a text style. The whole system lists every text style on the page, grouped into roles.
  • Color: click an element for its color, or switch to Pixel and hover the page to see the color under the pointer, then click. Luci's own panels are never picked.
  • Spacing: hover to see padding and margin, click an element for all its spacing (or choose one value), or drag from one element to another to measure the distance between them.
  • Whole page: one file for the page you are on: its text styles, colors and spacing, plus every kind of component on it with its variants and states.

States done in JavaScript

Luci reads hover, focus and active states exactly, the way a real pointer sets them: a card that lifts when the link around it is hovered, an arrow that fades in, a hover done in JavaScript. While it does, Chrome shows a "debugging this browser" bar for a moment. The panel lists every state with ✓, "no change" (Luci checked: nothing changes) or "not detected" (Luci could not be sure). Under Capture by hand, All missing states is chosen: press Capture them, then put the component in each state Luci names (hover, focus or press it) during its 3-second countdown.

A whole section

With the Component tool, select a section of the page (press ↑ Wider until all of it is selected), then This section. You get a section file with its layout, and a file for each kind of component in it, each described once. Download .zip saves them all.

How it changes by width

After capturing a component or a section, press How it changes by width. Luci finds the site's breakpoints that style it, reads it at 2 or 3 widths (the page is resized for a moment, with Chrome's debugging bar), puts the page back, and adds a Responsive table to the file: what changes at each width, such as padding, text size or the number of columns.

Chrome's debugger

Luci uses Chrome's debugger to read hover, focus and active states exactly, and to resize the page for How it changes by width. It attaches the debugger only to the tab you are capturing, only while it reads, and detaches it straight after.

Chrome does not let an extension ask for the debugger later, so Luci asks for it when you install it, and Chrome shows its debugger warning then.

Checking…

Luci's switch turns this on or off: open Luci's settings and press Don't use the debugger or Use the debugger. While it is off, Luci never attaches the debugger: states come from the page's stylesheet rules, and widths from a hidden frame of the page.

One system for the whole site

Capture the text styles, colors or spacing with The whole system on two or more pages of a site, and add each to the basket. In the basket, Merge into site systems makes one file per kind for the site: each row says which pages use it, and Conflicts lists anything named the same but different between pages (a heading size, a brand color token), so you can decide which one the site means.

Check what was built against its design

Press Check in the bar. Choose the component's design file: one in the basket (capture it with Component, This one, and add it), or paste one. Then click the component that was built and press Check against the design. Luci captures it the same way and lists what matches and each difference: a style, a part, a state, motion, or an accessibility level that got worse. Untick what you accept, then Copy as a prompt for your coding agent. Check another keeps the same design.

Use your own tokens

Under any capture, Use my tokens rewrites the file onto your own design tokens: paste your custom properties once (--space-4: 16px;, --brand: …;) and Luci keeps them. Every value one of your tokens holds is written through it; the rest keep their values, without the site's own token names. Put the site's values back undoes it.

Compare two captures

In the basket, tick Compare on two items, such as two sites' buttons, then Compare the two: every value that differs, side by side, ready to copy.

Colours from a screenshot

For an app or a picture you cannot inspect, choose Colour, then Image: drop a screenshot on the card, paste one, or choose a file. Luci estimates its palette (background, text, accent, border, each with its share of the picture). Every value is a guess, and the file says so; text styles and spacing cannot be read from an image.

DESIGN.md for Google Stitch

Set File to DESIGN.md (Stitch) (on a capture, or in the menu) to copy, download and export the open DESIGN.md format Google Stitch reads: design tokens first, then sections on colors, type, layout, shapes and components. The words are filled in from what Luci measured; nothing is invented.

The basket

Press Add to basket after any capture. Basket in the bar keeps your picks across pages and sites until you empty it. Rename items, write a note for your agent under any item (it stays with that item in every export), remove them, export one, or Export all as one file.

Keys

  • ↑ ↓ widen or narrow the selection, while hovering or after clicking
  • Enter picks, and after clicking takes This one
  • Esc cancels, or closes the panel

Good to know

  • A bare image, video or canvas is greyed out: press ↑ to take the component around it. Its file describes how the image is shown; Luci never copies the image itself.
  • Chrome's own pages and the Web Store can't be captured by any extension.
  • After you update or reload Luci, reload the page and click Luci's icon again.
  • Luci's bar covering something? Drag it by its edge or Luci's head. It stays there on that site; Right or Left in the menu puts it back.

Take Luci home

This extension is the part of Luci that travels. In your own project, Luci also takes notes on your running app, plans layouts on the live page, and remembers your project's rules and decisions for your coding agent. Free and open source. Nothing leaves your machine.

  1. Install Luci on your computer, once. It needs Node.js 18 or later. The install command comes with Luci's first release; until then, Luci runs from its source.
  2. In your project's folder: luci init. Say yes to the dev toolbar.
  3. luci serve, then open your app on this machine. Luci waits in the bottom corner.
